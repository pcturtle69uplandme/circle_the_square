---
The spine

Every good trailer has one idea. Yours is the dissolve: a real building, photographed, drains of colour and resolves into the plan — and the plan is where you play. Reality becomes schematic, and the schematic is somehow more alive than the photograph. You cut back and forth across that line for sixty seconds.

The tension isn't guns. It's access. A locked gate, a name you can't prove, a badge you haven't earned. Shoot bureaucracy like a heist and it plays as a thriller — which is exactly the joke Exam Administration Mothership is already making.

Style anchors — paste into every prompt

Consistency is the whole battle with image generators. Keep two registers and never blend them mid-shot.

REGISTER A — THE REAL (photoreal, use with your own photos as reference/img2img)
photorealistic architectural photography, modern triangular office building,
glass curtain wall and pale stone, overcast Northern European morning light,
cool neutral grade, muted teal-grey sky, no lens flare, no people's faces
visible, shot on 35mm, deep focus, restrained and documentary

REGISTER B — THE PLAN (the game's own look)
flat vector top-down orthographic floor plan, viewed from directly overhead,
no perspective, bone-white background #F4F3EF, ink-navy walls #1B2330,
amber door swings #E8A000, orange stair cores #E88F1F, cyan lift cores #22B8CC,
crisp thin linework, flat colour blocks with no outlines, no gradients,
no text labels, architectural drawing crossed with clean mobile-game art
